CITY GRAND PRIX

Five aircraft race three laps of an 1,800-unit circuit threaded between the blocks of a city after dark: towers wear windows that go on and off all evening, the rooftops carry beacons that blink out of phase, traffic runs the streets below while fliers cross the canyons above, and neon signs hang over the streets — and drift down them. The lap is generated, and so is everything on it — painted arrows that shove you up the road, ramps that span the whole width and put every car in the air, and floating powerups: a boost, or a shield that eats one wall. The lap starts on the ground and then leaves it. Out of the north-east corner a ramp lifts you clear of the traffic at 12%; the road bends west and winds a full turn around a tower, a corkscrew of 30 units radius, banked into the turn like a real spiral ramp and climbed one storey at a time; and the top of it opens into a glass tube you pass under a portal frame to enter — a roofed, level run at thirty units with the city either side of you. The whole south straight is the way back down. The barriers are there at every height, and the profile is gentle enough that you can hold the throttle over every crest — a hill only throws you when its curvature beats gravity, and none of them do. Hold the throttle, brake for the chicane, and try not to be the one who parks a starfighter in a wall. A controller works too — left stick or d-pad to steer, RT or A for throttle, LT to brake and reverse, right stick to look around, Start to restart. Drag the canvas to look, too — or press Steer: drag and a thumb anywhere on the screen sets the lock, with a pull up or down for throttle and brake. On a touch screen the look stick above the throttle does what the right stick does, and the results board is also a button: tap it to race again. P pauses.

      The five models arrive as 51.5 MB of glTF and 791,380 triangles; an offline pass keeps each hull's original
      paint and UVs and writes the lot as one 2.3 MB binary plus its textures. 16,000 triangles per hull rather than
      8,000, because at the old budget the clustering welded away 43% of the aircraft's surface — all the greebles
      smaller than a grid cell — and a hull that has lost its panels reads as a hull with a distorted texture. The
      pass reports the area it kept, and the loader flips the V axis the way glTF needs. Driving runs on a closed
      spline: speed decides how much steering authority you keep and how hard a corner throws you wide, so a corner
      taken flat out puts you in the wall, and a ramp taken flat out puts you in the air.
