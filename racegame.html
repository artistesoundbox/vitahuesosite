CITY GRAND PRIX

Five aircraft race three laps of a long circuit threaded between the blocks of a city that is awake: towers wear randomly lit windows, the rooftops carry beacons, masts and water tanks, and neon signs hang over the streets. The lap is generated, and so is everything on it — painted arrows that shove you up the road, ramps that put you in the air if you hit them fast, and floating powerups: a boost, or a shield that eats one wall. Hold the throttle, brake for the chicane, and try not to be the one who parks a starfighter in a wall. A controller works too — left stick or d-pad to steer, RT or A for throttle, LT to brake and reverse, right stick to look around, Y for the ▤ view, X for the palette, Start to restart. Drag the canvas to look, too. And ▤ ASCII view (or V) redraws the whole city as text: each texel becomes a brightness, looked up in a character ramp, so a tower turns into a wall of #.

      The five models arrive as 51.5 MB of glTF and 791,380 triangles; an offline pass keeps each hull's original
      paint and UVs and writes the lot as one 2.3 MB binary plus its textures. 16,000 triangles per hull rather than
      8,000, because at the old budget the clustering welded away 43% of the aircraft's surface — all the greebles
      smaller than a grid cell — and a hull that has lost its panels reads as a hull with a distorted texture. The
      pass reports the area it kept, and the loader flips the V axis the way glTF needs. Driving runs on a closed
      spline: speed decides how much steering authority you keep and how hard a corner throws you wide, so a corner
      taken flat out puts you in the wall, and a ramp taken flat out puts you in the air.
